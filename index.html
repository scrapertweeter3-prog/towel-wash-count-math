The washer kills the towel before the towel wears out

A $34 bath towel that survives 500 washes costs 6.8 cents per wash. A $12 towel that survives 120 costs 10 cents. The label never prints the wash count, so the rack sells on pile height and price, and the cheap towel wins the rack every time. But a towel dies in a hot dryer long before the weave gives out, and heat is the one variable you control for free.

Wash the $12 towel cold, skip the dryer sheet, pull it damp and air-dry it, and the failure clock slows to maybe 200 washes, which drops the cost to 6 cents and flips the ranking. Same towel, same price, one habit. The durable towel is not a product you buy. It is a wash setting you already own.

Hang-dry math: a household running 3 loads a week spends roughly $1.50 a load drying, near $234 a year, and every load that comes out damp-hung pays that bill at zero. Heat is also the variable that snaps elastic and seals stains. Cold wash plus air dry extends fabric life and deletes most of the drying bill with the same motion.
